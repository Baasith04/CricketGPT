# Duckworth-Lewis-Stern (DLS) Method

The Duckworth-Lewis-Stern (DLS) method is the standard mathematical system used in limited-overs cricket—including the Indian Premier League (IPL)—to calculate revised target scores and determine winners in matches shortened by rain, bad light, or other interruptions.

## Core Principles

1. **Two Remaining Resources:** A batting team's scoring potential depends on two resources remaining in its innings:
   - **Overs remaining** (balls left to be bowled)
   - **Wickets remaining** (wickets in hand, from 10 down to 0)
2. **Resource Percentage Table:** At any interruption or resumption, the DLS software calculates what percentage of total batting resources each team had available across its scheduled innings.
3. **Par Score and Target Calculation:**
   - If the chasing team (Team 2) receives fewer resources than Team 1, Team 2's target is scaled down proportionally to the ratio of resources available.
   - If Team 1's innings was interrupted mid-way and Team 2 gets more resources, Team 2's target is scaled up based on Team 1's lost overs/resources (using the Stern "G50" and scoring-rate adjustments in modern T20s).
   - If a match is abandoned during the chase after the minimum number of overs (5 overs per side in IPL league/playoff regulation matches) have been completed, the **DLS Par Score** at the point of abandonment decides the winner: if Team 2 is ahead of the Par Score by 1 run or more, Team 2 wins; if tied with Par Score, the match is a tie.

# IPL Key Playing Conditions

- **Powerplay (Overs 1–6):** In a standard 20-over IPL innings, the first 6 overs constitute the mandatory Powerplay, during which a maximum of 2 fielders are permitted outside the 30-yard circle.
- **Super Over:** Tied IPL matches are decided by a one-over eliminator (Super Over). If the Super Over itself is tied, consecutive Super Overs are played until a winner is determined.
- **Minimum Overs for a Result:** Normally at least 5 overs per team must be bowled for a result to be declared in a rain-affected IPL match (including DLS calculation).
